Sound Advice — music credits
============================

Everything except the two files below is generated by code inside the
app (filtered noise), so there is nothing to credit and nothing to
license.

Both tracks were loudness-matched before use — see "Loudness" below.
The same credits are shown on the app's Credits screen, which is what
actually discharges the CC BY attribution requirement; this file is the
fuller record.

  Title      : Ballade No. 1 in G minor, Op. 23
  Composer   : Frédéric Chopin (1810–1849)
  Performer  : Frank Levy
  Album      : Musopen: The Complete Works of Frédéric Chopin (2015)
  Source URL : https://musopen.org
  Licence    : Public domain recording

  The composition has been out of copyright for well over a century,
  but that is not what makes this usable — a modern recording of an old
  piece is its own copyrighted work. This recording comes from Musopen's
  crowdfunded project to record Chopin and release the RECORDINGS free
  of copyright. Confirm the licence line on the Musopen download page
  and keep a screenshot.

  ** UNRESOLVED: check this before you launch **

  The Free Music Archive page states CC BY, and that personal and
  commercial use are allowed. But the licence tag embedded inside the
  MP3 file itself says something different:

      Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 United States
      http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/us/

  Those are not the same licence. The embedded tag dates from 2015 and
  is quite possibly stale metadata that FMA's current page supersedes.
  Click "More details" on the FMA page, see what it says, and screenshot
  it for your records. Then correct the line above if needed, and the
  matching entry in js/app.js (MUSIC_CREDITS).

  Either way you are crediting the artist, which is what both licences
  ask for first.


Loudness
--------
Both tracks are normalised to −18.0 LUFS, the same target the eight
generated sounds are rendered to. Without that, a difference in
concentration between two sounds could just be a difference in volume.

The correction is a single fixed gain, not compression, so the music
keeps its own dynamics. That is deliberate: the Chopin has a loudness
range of about 19 LU against roughly 14 for the song, and flattening
that would make it stop being classical music. Worth stating in the
write-up as a known difference between the two music conditions.

Both files use identical encoder settings (MP3, VBR ~62 kbps, 32 kHz,
stereo) so that audio quality cannot differ between the two conditions
either.

If you replace either track, re-run the loudness match — do not just
drop a new file in. Measure it, work out the gain needed to reach
−18 LUFS, and apply that before saving. And do it BEFORE launch: once
people start testing, changing a sound makes scores from different
weeks incomparable.
