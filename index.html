What I can see

This app is part of my science fair project, so I look at how people use it. Being straight with you about exactly what that means:

I can see

Your nickname · which sounds you pick · how long each study session lasts · the rating you give it · your concentration test scores.

I can’t see

Your real name · your email · what you were actually studying · anything else on your phone. There is no microphone access and no location.

Everything gets combined with everyone else’s and reported as totals and averages. You can delete all of your data from Settings at any time, and you can stop using it whenever you want without telling me.

Want to find your sound?

There’s a concentration test built in. It runs you through four different sounds and tells you which one you actually focus best with.

The full test

About 12 min

Four sounds, three minutes each. You get a proper ranking at the end. Best done somewhere quiet with headphones on.

Not now? Completely fine — you can start studying straight away and test one sound at a time whenever you feel like it.

Sound check

Two quick things, so the test measures your concentration and not your phone settings.

1. Turn off silent mode

The switch on the side of your iPhone silences this app completely. If it’s on, you’d be doing every test in silence without knowing.

2. Set your volume

Play the tone and set your volume so it’s clearly audible but comfortable. Then leave it there for the whole test.

Headphones are strongly recommended — they keep the sound consistent and block out whatever is happening around you.

How it works

Pictures fade from one into the next.

Tap the screen every time you see a city.
Do nothing when you see mountains.

Mountains only show up now and then, which is what makes it hard — you get into a rhythm of tapping and have to catch yourself.

Go as fast as you can while staying accurate. Don’t worry about the odd mistake, everybody makes them.

On a laptop you can press the space bar instead of tapping.

Sounds & credits

Generated by code

White, pink and brown noise, rain, stream, ocean, forest and fireplace are all synthesised in your browser from filtered noise. No recordings are used, so there is nothing to license and nothing to download.

Every sound here — including the two music tracks — is matched to the same loudness (−18 LUFS, measured the way broadcasters do). Otherwise a result like “people concentrate better with white noise” could just mean white noise was louder.

The concentration test

Modelled on the Gradual Onset Continuous Performance Test (GradCPT), developed by the Boston Attention Lab and distributed by The Many Brains Project. This is an independent implementation of the paradigm, not that instrument. See Fortenbaugh et al. (2015), Psychological Science 26(9), 1497–1510.

Test photographs

Twenty free-licence photographs — ten cities, ten mountains — converted to grayscale and matched so that every one has the same brightness and contrast. Without that matching, a brighter photo would make its own trials easier than the rest, for reasons having nothing to do with the sound being tested. Photographers are listed in the scenes/CREDITS.txt file that ships with the app.
